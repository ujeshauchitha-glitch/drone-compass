# abstract_draft.md

> Generated by `experiments/make_abstract.py` from files in `results/`. Simulation only.

**Objective.** Under what conditions, if any, could a radical-pair (spin-chemical) magnetic compass improve the heading of an inertial attitude filter relative to a MEMS magnetometer? The study is simulation-only; no experiment exists and no new physics is claimed.

**Method.** A spin-dynamics yield model (two ¹⁴N couplings from the literature) feeds a photon-limited multi-element sensor and a Poisson maximum-likelihood axis estimator, whose output enters a 600 s quaternion attitude filter with gyro, accelerometer and a MEMS magnetometer, the radical-pair axis, or both, under shared magnetic disturbances (200 paired runs per configuration). Sensor, noise and distortion parameters are assumed and swept.

**Results (simulated).** The sensor observes only the field axis; element sets related by axis permutations are mirror-ambiguous (failure rate 0.71), and a non-orthogonal set resolves this (error/bound ratio 0.93–0.97). In the pre-registered comparison 0 of 390 configurations reduced yaw RMSE by at least 20% at both 60 s and 600 s, but the magnetometer baseline was inconsistent (unmodelled soft iron). With online calibration of hard-iron, soft-iron and motor-current fields, which lowers magnetometer yaw RMSE at 600 s from 0.22° to 0.06°, 12 of 270 configurations meet the criterion, 11 of them with a radical-pair error of 0.3° per axis against a 2 µT-noise magnetometer (about 46% at 600 s) or at 15° dip (21%–25%); at 0.5 µT and 45° dip the 60 s change is -9% and the 600 s change 10%. Reaching 0.3° needs roughly 3.1×10^5 (ideal contrast) to 2.6×10^9 (relaxation-limited) detected photons per element. In one-at-a-time sensitivity runs only dip angle and magnetometer noise lift the best-case benefit to 20% (none do for the pre-registered filters).

**Limits.** The filter failed its pre-registered consistency gate on the original trajectory (unmodelled acceleration); an amended gate passed post hoc. Attitude only, no position; isotropic Gaussian sensor errors; idealised calibration priors.

## Not claimed

* No sensor, measurement or flight exists; every number is a simulated result under assumed parameters (see `PLAN.md`, `report.pdf`).
* The pre-registered answer is NO (0 of 390). The calibrated study was specified in Amendment 3 after that answer was known and is reported next to it, not instead of it.
* The filter failed Gate S3(i); Gate S3(i)′ is post hoc and not an independent test.
* σ_rp = 0.3° is the smallest value in the grid, so the size of the benefit at smaller σ_rp is not known.
* Photon numbers use the median-direction Cramér–Rao bound and an ambiguity-failure threshold; the worst field direction needs far more (see `results/stage2_requirements.csv`).
