**INTERIM abstract (Stages 0–2 only; Stage 3 is planned and no navigation results exist).**

**Objective.** We ask under what conditions, if any, a radical-pair (spin-chemical) magnetic compass could aid heading in GNSS-denied inertial navigation compared with a conventional MEMS magnetometer. The study is simulation-only; no experiment exists and no new physics is claimed.

**Method.** A singlet-yield model of a flavin-like radical pair (two ¹⁴N hyperfine couplings with tensors taken from Hiscock et al. 2016) feeds a photon-limited sensor of K identical oriented elements, a Poisson maximum-likelihood axis estimator and Cramér–Rao bounds. Contrast, noise and ensemble-order parameters are assumed and swept; every assumed value is labelled.

**Results (simulated).** The minimal model's yield anisotropy is 0.206, but an assumed richer hyperfine model gives 0.010, and relaxation at 10^7 s⁻¹ leaves only 1.5× our pre-registered 10^-3 threshold. The yield is symmetric under field reversal, so only the field axis is observable. Because both nitrogen tensors share one principal frame, the molecule has an exact sign-flip symmetry, and any array of elements related by axis permutations has a four-fold mirror ambiguity (failure rate 0.71 at every photon number; the pre-registered gate failed). A non-orthogonal three-element set, chosen by a pre-registered distinguishability rule, removes it in the minimal model (median-error to Cramér–Rao ratio 0.93–0.97, slope -0.52 on fresh data). With an assumed 5° tilt of one tensor frame the mirror images become separable above about 4.6×10^6 photons in the minimal model, but with an assumed 15° tilt in a richer model about 1.7×10^8 photons are needed. For a derived contrast factor of 0.044 (richer model), a 5° median error needs about 8.4×10^5 detected photons per element by the Cramér–Rao bound, about 3.2×10^7 for at most 5% ambiguity failures, and about 3.8×10^10 for the worst field direction. With Ren et al.'s phenomenological signal, our bound gives 6.8×10^7 (default) and 4.6×10^6 (optimised) total photons for 5° (their published figures: 5.3×10^7–2.3×10^8 and 3.94×10^5; a factor of 12 remains for the optimised row).

**Planned.** Stage 3, an attitude-filter comparison against a MEMS magnetometer under shared magnetic disturbances, has not been run; no claim about navigation benefit is made.
