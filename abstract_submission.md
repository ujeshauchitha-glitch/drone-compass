## Abstract (full, 319 words)

**Objectives.** To determine under what conditions a radical-pair (spin-chemical) magnetic compass could improve attitude and heading estimation for GNSS-denied inertial navigation relative to a MEMS magnetometer, and what sensor performance that would require. The work is simulation-only: no sensor was built, sensor parameters are assumed and swept, and no new physics is claimed.

**Results (simulated).** A spin-dynamics yield model feeds a photon-limited multi-element sensor, a maximum-likelihood axis estimator and a quaternion attitude filter (200 paired runs per configuration). Against an uncalibrated magnetometer, none of 390 pre-registered configurations gave a 20% yaw-error reduction at both 60 s and 600 s; that baseline proved inconsistent. With online calibration of hard-iron, soft-iron and motor-current fields (magnetometer yaw RMSE at 600 s falls from 0.22° to 0.06°), 12 of 270 configurations meet the criterion, 11 of them where the magnetometer noise is 2 µT (about 46% at 600 s) or the field dip is 15° (21%–25%) and the radical-pair sensor reaches 0.3° per axis. At 0.5 µT and 45° dip the changes are -9% (60 s) and 10% (600 s).

**Conclusions.** With the magnetic disturbance shared by both sensors, a radical-pair channel is not useful against a good, calibrated magnetometer. It could matter against a poor magnetometer or at low dip, and only at sub-degree accuracy, which needs roughly 3.1×10^5 to 2.6×10^9 detected photons per element depending on the assumed contrast.

**Key innovative steps.** An axis-only, sign-invariant measurement model; the identification of a mirror ambiguity caused by molecular symmetry (failure rate 0.71 for axis-permuted elements) and its removal by a selected non-orthogonal element set; identical disturbance and calibration for both sensors, with no immunity credited; and a pre-registered protocol whose failed checks are reported.

**Significance.** The study turns a qualitative bio-inspired idea into measurable targets, namely angular accuracy and contrast, before any hardware is built, and indicates that magnetometer calibration is the stronger lever under these assumptions. Attitude only; position is not evaluated.

## Abstract (short fallback if the portal has a tight limit, 128 words)

We ask when a radical-pair (spin-chemical) magnetic compass could improve attitude estimation for GNSS-denied inertial navigation relative to a MEMS magnetometer. In simulation only (no sensor built; parameters assumed), a spin-yield model, photon-limited estimator and quaternion filter were compared over 390 pre-registered and 270 calibrated configurations. None of the pre-registered configurations gave a 20% yaw-error reduction at both 60 s and 600 s (the baseline was inconsistent). With online magnetometer calibration, 12 of 270 did, mainly with a noisy 2 µT magnetometer or 15° dip and a radical-pair sensor at 0.3° per axis. Under shared disturbance the channel is not useful against a good calibrated magnetometer. Key steps: axis-only sign-invariant fusion, a symmetry-induced mirror ambiguity and its removal, and a pre-registered protocol. Significance: measurable sensor targets before hardware.
